# A palette of its own: a sun on the horizon

`41c65bed9` · 2026-08-06

Amber earns its place three times over — it is CRT phosphor, it is desert, and
it is a warm ground in a category that is wall to wall blue. Six colours: a
core light, an amber, an ember, sandstone, a deep brown, and the ground it all
sits on.

The surfaces are near-black with warmth in them rather than brown, because a
brown editor is tiring within the hour: what should be warm is the light
falling on it, not the paper. The syntax colours are chosen against that
ground rather than borrowed from the dark theme, whose blue call on an amber
background is the one pairing that looks like a mistake — keywords take the
ember, strings a desert green, calls a pale gold, and the work is done by
lightness rather than by hue.

The terminal has a matching scheme, since half of this app is a terminal and
one left blue would have been the odd rectangle on screen. ANSI still means
what ANSI means — red is red, and a failing test has to look like one — but
blue becomes sand, which is the only colour that could not stay.

Two things this needed on the way. Themes are compared by name now: the switch
asked whether light had changed, which two dark palettes answer the same way,
so choosing this one did nothing. And `--theme` fixes the palette for a
capture, so the documentation's pictures do not depend on whichever theme the
machine taking them is set to.
